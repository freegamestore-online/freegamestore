What the caps actually mean

We chose caps that fit normal game patterns — solo play, small lobbies, occasional bursts. The vast majority of games never bump into them. The caps exist for the rare case where a single game suddenly handles thousands of concurrent players — and where the underlying CF cost would otherwise scale into four-figure-monthly territory overnight.

Why we cap multiplayer hard. Real-time WebSocket rooms run on Cloudflare Durable Objects, billed per GB-second of duration. A single popular multiplayer game with 10,000 concurrent players 24/7 would cost us roughly $41,000/month in DO charges alone. The 50-player-hours/day cap keeps that worst case bounded. If your game legitimately needs more players, that's exactly when Pro pays off — for both of us.

How we count costs

Most things on Cloudflare are basically free at the scale of small games. The hard cliff is Durable Objects (multiplayer rooms). Here's what we actually pay:

  • Static hosting (CF Pages): $0 — even at high traffic. CF doesn't charge for asset egress.
  • Workers requests: 100k/day per Worker on the free tier; Workers Paid ($5/mo) covers 10M/mo.
  • D1 (the database): 5M reads, 100k writes, 5GB free per day.
  • KV (per-player saves): 100k reads + 1k writes/day free; cents per million beyond.
  • Durable Objects (multiplayer): $0.15 per million requests + $12.50 per million GB-seconds duration. This is the line item the cap protects.
  • Workers AI (NPCs, procgen): per-token billing; only available on Pro.

Why publish all this

Most game platforms hide costs behind marketing copy. We don't. If you're going to build on top of FreeGameStore, you deserve to know exactly what's free, what we pay for, and where the upgrade prompt will appear. Pre-launch we have no real users — these caps and prices are our best guess and may shift before launch. We'll never silently move a free feature behind the paywall.
